# Home and offer copy changes, 2026-11-02

Source: the funnel audit on branch `claude/funnel-audit` (`docs/audit/FUNNEL_AUDIT_2026-11-01.md`). Only hand-maintained pages were edited: `index.html`, `offer.html`, `offer/index.html`, `agents.html`. No generated town, region or permit page, nothing under `functions/`, `.github/` or `data/`, and `js/promo-switch.js` is untouched (every code still `false`).

Notation: where the old text had an em dash it is written `[em dash]`. The founder's personal mailbox is referred to by role, not written out.

Each change has an ID so it can be accepted or rejected on its own. They are independent except where noted.

## index.html (home)

### H1. Hero paragraph: speak to follow-on trades and suppliers

- Before: `When a building permit is filed on a home, the owner is spending on it <em>right now</em>, and your trade is next. We collect building permits from the Massachusetts towns we cover, ...`
- After: `A building permit is a dated public record of approved work at a real address. The trade named on it is usually already hired. The trades and suppliers who come after are not: insulation, drywall, flooring, cabinets, roofing, solar, sitework, and the lumber and supply yards that stock the job. We collect building permits from the Massachusetts towns we cover, ...` (rest of the sentence unchanged)
- Why: for the trade named on a permit the job is usually already sold, which is the first objection a contractor raises. The value is for the trades and suppliers who sell to the job after the permit. "On a home" and "the owner is spending" also overclaimed (the file includes commercial permits).

### H2. New FAQ answer: "isn't the job already sold?"

- Before: (no such question)
- After, question: `If a permit is filed, isn't the job already sold?`
- After, answer: `Often, for the trade named on the permit. The rest of the job usually is not. An addition or a gut remodel still needs insulation, drywall, flooring and cabinets after the framing permit. A new house needs sitework, a roof and often solar. Every one of those jobs buys lumber and materials from a supply yard. The file gives you the address, project type and date, plus the permit holder (usually the contractor or applicant) where the town lists one, so you can reach the job or the contractor who pulled it before the next trade is picked.`
- Why: answers the objection plainly and names the follow-on trades and supply yards. Placed before "What do I actually get?". It says "permit holder ... where the town lists one" and never promises a homeowner name.

### H3. Personal mailbox replaced with the business inbox

- Before: `Already subscribed? Your leads are in your Monday email [em dash] the Download your leads button in it always serves the current file. Can't find it? <a href="mailto:(founder's personal mailbox)?subject=...">Email me</a> and I'll send your link.`
- After: `Already subscribed? Your leads are in your Monday email. The Download your leads button in it always serves the current file. Can't find it? <a href="mailto:hello@masspermits.com?subject=...">Email hello@masspermits.com</a> and we'll send your link.`
- Why: hello@masspermits.com is the published business inbox already used in the home page footer, the FAQ, the offer pages, agents.html and the Organization schema. Keeps a personal address off the home page. The em dash in the same sentence is gone too.

### H4. "BEST VALUE" badge removed

- Before: `Weekly Feed <span ...>BEST VALUE</span>`
- After: `Weekly Feed`
- Why: there is one plan, so the badge compares against nothing and reads as sales copy.

### H5. Dormant FOUNDER2026 promo markup removed

- Before: the `<template data-promo="FOUNDER2026">` founders banner (`Founders' rate: first month of the Weekly Feed for $49. Applied automatically when you start here.`), its `mpPromo` call, the pricing-box template (`Founders' rate: first month $49 when you start from the banner at the top`) and its call, the `<script src="/js/promo-switch.js">` include, the `STRIPE_LINK_FOUNDER` constant (a checkout link with `prefilled_promo_code=FOUNDER2026`) and the `#buy-banner` wiring in `wireBuyButtons()`.
- After: all gone. A head comment notes the page carries no promotion markup; the `wireBuyButtons()` comment now says the hero button is the only one enriched.
- Why: policy is $99 a month only, no promos. With the markup deleted, a one-line edit to the switch can no longer put a promo on the page.

### H6. Sample paragraph: no hard-coded count

- Before (visible): `Drop your email for a free sample file: around a thousand recent Massachusetts permits, filterable by trade and town, that opens in any browser.`
- After (visible): `Drop your email for a free sample file: recent Massachusetts permits, filterable by trade and town, that opens in any browser.`
- Before (HTML comment above it): `It is a ~1,000-lead browser file covering ~45 towns and every trade ...`
- After (HTML comment): no numbers; says the sample build sets the size and town list, and that a number typed here drifts (it once said ~45 towns while the coverage page said 91 sources).
- Why: the sample size and town count are set by the sample build, not this file, so any number here goes stale. The live count is already computed and shown in the "On file" block from `data.json`, and per-town coverage is on `/research/coverage`.

### O2. Dormant FIRST90 promo markup removed

- Before: `data-promo-off="FIRST90"` on the tag, h1, buy button and price chip; three `<template data-promo="FIRST90">` blocks (`Limited offer · code FIRST90 auto-applied`, `$99 $9.90`, `Start for $9.90 [em dash] then $99/mo`, `$9.90 month one`) with their `mpPromo` calls; the switch include.

## offer/index.html

### F1. Headline

- Before: `This week, homeowners across MA are pulling permits. Get those permits in one file every Monday.`
- After: `This week, Massachusetts towns are issuing building permits. Get those permits in one file every Monday.`
- Why: not every permit is pulled by a homeowner (contractors pull most, and commercial permits are in the file).

### F2. Lead sentence

- Before: `A building permit means a property owner was just approved for work on their property.`
- After: `A building permit is a dated public record of approved work at a real address.`
- Why: same as O1.

### F3. First card

- Before: `Funded intent` / `A permit means the homeowner is approved and about to spend. Not a tire-kicker [em dash] a real, dated project.`
- After: `Real, dated work` / `A permit is approved work at a real address, with a date. The trade named on it is usually hired already; the trades and suppliers who come next are not.`
- Why: "about to spend" is the same overclaim as the old home hero, and the line had an em dash. The new line carries the follow-on trade point.

### F4. Dormant FIRST90 promo markup removed

- Before: two `<div data-promo-off="FIRST90">` wrappers; two templates (`New customer offer`, `$99 $9.90 first month`, `90% off your first month, then $99/mo`, `Start for $9.90`, `Get this week's leads [em dash] $9.90`, `then $99/mo · cancel anytime · month-one money-back`) with their `mpPromo` calls; the switch include.
- After: the wrappers are plain `<div>`s holding the same full-price blocks; templates, calls and include gone. The CTA ids `cta1` and `cta2` and the `/offer/click` beacon are unchanged, so `/ops` counts are unaffected.
- Why: $99 a month only.

## agents.html

### A1. "The name on the permit"

- Before: `Full property address, postcard- and door-knock-ready, plus the name on the permit where the town lists one`
- After: `Full property address, postcard- and door-knock-ready, plus the permit holder (usually the contractor or applicant) where the town lists one`

## test/promo-switch.test.mjs

- Before: required each page to load the switch and carry at least six promo templates, and checked the promo text appears when a code is switched on.
- After: the switch-file tests and the `_headers` test are kept as they were. The page tests now require that no page loads the switch or carries `<template>`, `data-promo`, `mpPromo`, a `prefilled_promo_code` link, the founders banner wiring, or promo or "best value" wording, and that each checkout button id is present once.
- Why: the old tests asserted the markup that this change removes. `node --test` over `test/` passes except `index concurrency: two interleaved writers both land`, which fails the same way on unmodified main.

## Not changed here, and why

### Dead link `/permits/west-end-electrical` (for the private engine)

- Where: the "Electrical in West End (8)" chip on `electrical-leads-massachusetts.html`, a page the refresh bot regenerates, so it is not edited here (the next refresh would overwrite the fix anyway).
- Problem: no `permits/west-end-electrical.html` is generated, so the chip 404s.
- Fix in the engine's regional-page template: emit a town chip only when the target `permits/<slug>-<trade>.html` page is generated in the same build (or map West End, a Boston neighbourhood, to `/permits/boston-electrical`, which exists). A link check over the built output before commit would catch any repeat.
- Not done: no `_redirects` file was added; that would be a new production routing rule and is the owner's call.

### Stale or overclaiming lines outside the allowed scope

- "Boston, Cape Cod, the South Shore", "~45 towns", "the name on the permit" and "every competitor's ... value" are in the sample and nurture emails under `functions/api/` (`nurture.js`, `request-sample.js`, `agent-sample.js`, `newsletter.js`, `newsletter-send.js`). None of these phrases is on a hand-maintained page. `functions/` is off limits for this change; the audit (changes 7, 8, 14) has the proposed wording.
- Generated pages: "Each is a homeowner who just got approved for work" and the permit-holder line without "where the town lists one" (audit change 9) belong in the engine's templates.
- The Stripe product description still promises homeowner names (audit change 1). It is set in Stripe, outside this repo.

### Left as they were on purpose

- Existing em dashes in lines this change does not otherwise touch (for example `Start the Monday feed [em dash] $99/mo`, `See the data first [em dash] free sample`, the offer page's guarantee line `[em dash] Pat`). They are not stale or overclaiming, so they were left to keep the diff to the requested fixes. They can be swept in a follow-up.
- "How it works" step 3 (audit change 6, naming suppliers) was not rewritten. The hero and the new FAQ answer carry that point.
- `js/promo-switch.js` and its `_headers` rule stay, with every code false. No page loads the file any more.
