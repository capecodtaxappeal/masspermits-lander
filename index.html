Questions

If a permit is filed, isn't the job already sold?

Often, for the trade named on the permit. The rest of the job usually is not. An addition or a gut remodel still needs insulation, drywall, flooring and cabinets after the framing permit. A new house needs sitework, gutters and often solar. Every one of those jobs buys lumber and materials from a supply yard. The file gives you the address, project type and date, plus the permit holder (usually the contractor or applicant) where the town lists one, so you can reach the job, or the contractor who pulled the permit, before the next trade is picked.

Which towns are in it?

Massachusetts cities and towns from Cape Cod and the Islands to Western Massachusetts. Not every town publishes permit records we can include, so check yours before you pay: type it into the sample form above, or see every town in the file, with its dates.

How fresh is it?

A new file arrives every Monday. Most towns publish as they issue permits. Some publish monthly or less often, so their permits arrive in batches when the town publishes. Every row shows its permit date, so you always know how old a lead is.

What if my town is missing?

Then check before you pay, not after: the sample form shows which of your towns are in the file. Look at the towns around it too, since the subscription is the whole statewide file and many trades work a radius. Leave your towns in the form either way; it tells us where people want coverage. New towns show up on the coverage page and in the Monday file as they are added.

Do I get homeowner names?

No. There is no homeowner name column. Where the town lists one, the file carries the permit holder, which is usually the contractor or whoever applied for the permit.

What exactly do I get?

Every Monday, by email: a dashboard that opens in your web browser (search it, filter by trade and town, sort by project value) and spreadsheets, one master CSV and one per trade. Each row has the full street address, the permit date and the project type, plus the town's description of the work, the permit holder and the declared value where the town publishes them. The email's download button always serves the current file.

What does it cost, and can I cancel?

$99 a month for the whole statewide file, every trade and every town we cover. It is month to month with no contract, and you can cancel anytime by writing to hello@masspermits.com. Files you already received are yours to keep. The sample is free and needs no card.

What if the leads don't pan out?

Every lead is a real, verifiable public permit. If your first paid month doesn't return more than it cost, email hello@masspermits.com and I'll refund it: no forms, no argument. Cancel anytime; any leads already delivered are yours to keep. Pat

Where do the permits come from?

Public municipal permit records. Every row shows its town and date, so you can check any permit against the town's own records.
