Accessory dwelling units in Massachusetts permit records

Massachusetts legalised accessory dwelling units by right in 2025, and permit records are the first place the effect would show. We found 136 ADU permits across 18 towns. The monthly counts climb steeply - 1, 4, 15, 28, 37, 37, 14 - which looks exactly like a boom.

It is not one. Those counts rise because we were adding municipal sources over the same period, not because Massachusetts was approving more ADUs. Normalized against all permits from the same 20 sources, the rate per 10,000 permits peaks at 72.3 in 2026-04 and finishes at 38.9 - flat, noisy, and if anything declining across the months with the largest raw counts. This is the one analysis on this site that nobody outside it could reproduce, because it requires knowing our own source-onboarding history.

Mostly new building, not conversion

34 of 136 ADU-matching permits (25.0%) mention a garage somewhere in the description. That is worth knowing, but on its own it says nothing about whether a garage is being converted or built - and reading it as conversion is exactly the mistake this section originally made.

Matching conversion language and new-construction language separately: 57 permits describe new construction against 31 that describe a conversion. Among the garage-mentioning permits it is 14 to 14, which is too close to call. 48 of 136 permits (35%) use neither vocabulary and are left unclassified rather than assigned to whichever side would make a cleaner sentence.

So the useful signal for a framing, electrical or HVAC contractor is the opposite of the intuitive one: an ADU permit in these records is more often a new structure going up than a garage or basement being finished.

How this was measured

Question

How many accessory dwelling units appear in Massachusetts building-permit records, and is the trend real?

Rows used

Rows whose description is at least 8 characters, matched against \baccessory dwelling|\badu\b|in[- ]law (apartment|suite)|accessory apartment. 136 matched. The rate denominator is every permit from the same sources in the same month, and months with fewer than 200 rows are dropped as too thin to carry a rate.

Sources included

20 of 31 sources - those publishing usable free text on at least 20% of rows.

Sources excluded, and why

11 sources that publish category labels rather than descriptions. An ADU is invisible in those records, so including them in the denominator would deflate the rate with permits that could never match.

Known limitation

136 rows is a small sample, and the matching rule will catch a permit that merely mentions an in-law apartment as context. The negative result - that the normalized rate is flat - is more robust than any single monthly figure.

Questions

How many ADU permits are there in Massachusetts?

136 accessory-dwelling-unit permits are legible in the 20 Massachusetts municipal sources that publish work descriptions, across 18 towns. That is a floor, not a census: towns publishing only category labels cannot show an ADU at all.

Is ADU permitting growing in Massachusetts?

Not measurably, in this data. Raw monthly counts rise from 1 to 37, but that tracks sources being added. Per 10,000 permits from the same sources the rate stays between 30.8 and 72.3 with no trend.

What kind of ADUs are being built?

25.0% of the matching permits also mention a garage, making garage conversion the most common identifiable form in these records.
