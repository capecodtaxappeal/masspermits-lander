The Friday gap in Massachusetts building-permit records

Massachusetts towns publish building permits on a weekly rhythm that nobody designed. Across 37,622 dated permits from 31 municipal sources, Friday carries 12.4% of the records - 60% of the next-quietest weekday. In Hingham, across 900 weekday permits spanning 12 calendar Fridays, the count is exactly zero.

This is about what towns publish, not what they do. The date on a permit record is the date the town wrote it down, and the data proves it: 697 permits in this dataset are dated to a Saturday or Sunday, across 20 of 31 sources. Building departments are not issuing permits at the weekend. That column is a recording date, and every number below should be read as a fact about municipal record-keeping.

The week, as the records tell it

Monday through Thursday sit within a couple of points of each other - 21.4%, 22.4%, 21.3% and 20.7% respectively. Then Friday drops to 12.4%. It is not a gentle taper; it is a step down.

Is this just one vendor’s software?

It is a fair objection, and the answer decides whether this is a fact about Massachusetts or about a piece of software. 21 of 31 sources here run the same permit vendor. Split the data on it and the dip is on both sides: those 21 towns file 10.7% of permits on a Friday against a 21.8% Monday-to-Thursday average, and the 10 sources on entirely separate systems - including Boston, Worcester and Cambridge, which each run their own - file 13.7% against 21.2%. All 10 of those 10 independent sources sit below their own weekday average. The shared-vendor towns dip harder (0.49 of their weekday average against 0.65), so some of this probably is the software - but removing them entirely does not remove the pattern.

One caveat, and it is ours not the towns’. Our fetcher stops at 900 newest records per source, and 14 of 31 sources are sitting on exactly that number. For those towns the permit count below is a ceiling, not a census, and the earliest date is where our pull stopped rather than where the town’s records begin. It does not move the result: the Friday figure is a share of each source’s own rows, and taking the newest N records from a source does not change which weekday they fall on. But a count that is really a cap should say so.

Hingham: a town with no Fridays

Hingham publishes on 49 distinct days between 2026-05-27 and 2026-08-20, carrying 900 weekday permits - which is our 900-record ceiling, so that window is the slice we hold rather than the whole of Hingham’s history. None of them is a Friday, and the window contains 12 of them. The most likely explanation is mundane - a clerk who batches the week's entries, or a system that stamps records on the day they are keyed rather than the day they are approved. It is still the clearest illustration in the dataset of why a permit date is a filing artifact.

Where Friday survives, and where it does not

The spread between towns is wider than the national pattern suggests. Boston files 17.9% of its permits on a Friday; Reading manages 2.7%.

Why this matters if you sell to homeowners

If you work permit records as leads, the practical consequence is that a Friday pull is the thinnest of the week almost everywhere, and Monday and Tuesday carry the backlog. That is a scheduling fact about town halls, not about demand.

How this was measured

Question

How are Massachusetts building permits distributed across the days of the week, as published?

Rows used

All 37,622 rows in the dataset that carry a parseable date. The per-source table shows every source with at least 100 weekday rows (31 of 31). The zero-Friday finding additionally requires a window spanning at least 6 calendar Fridays.

Sources included

31 municipal sources.

Sources excluded, and why

None. Every source that publishes a date is included; sources below the row threshold are counted in the totals but not listed individually, because a percentage on a handful of rows is noise.

Known limitation

The date column is a published or recorded date, not a verified municipal issuance timestamp - 697 weekend-dated rows across 20 sources prove it. This article measures publication behaviour and should not be read as a claim about when work was approved.

Evidence

Computed 2026-08-18 from a dataset fetched 2026-08-21. Download the figures behind this page (JSON).

Questions

Do Massachusetts towns really issue fewer permits on Fridays?

They publish fewer. Friday carries 12.4% of dated records against roughly 22.4% on Tuesday, but the same dataset contains 697 weekend-dated permits, which shows the date column records when a permit was entered rather than when it was approved.

Which Massachusetts town has no Friday permits at all?

Hingham. Across 900 weekday permits published on 49 distinct days between 2026-05-27 and 2026-08-20, none is dated to a Friday, in a window containing 12 of them.

Where does this data come from?
